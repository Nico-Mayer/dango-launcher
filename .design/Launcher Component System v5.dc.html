Component system

A keyboard-first launcher: one pill, one card, one view.

Dango is a SearchBar pill and, 8 px below it, one card that holds the view (list, grid, detail, form, progress view or empty view) and the ActionBar. Commands return data; Dango draws every surface. Every colour, radius, size and type face comes from one Theme global in ui/theme.slint.

Structure over style
Parts define layout and behaviour. Colour, radius and size are Theme values. Rust never passes a colour.
Keyboard first
In lists and grids the cursor stays in the SearchBar. ↑ ↓ move, ↵ runs the primary action, {{ k.panel }} opens the rest, Escape steps back.
Starts as a pill
The launcher opens as the bare pill. The card appears when results land, when something needs attention, or on ↓.
One primary action
The first action of the selection runs on ↵ and shows as the chip in the ActionBar. Every action is in the ActionPanel.
Commands bring data, not chrome
A command supplies items and actions; Dango draws the surfaces, so every command looks and behaves alike.

Themes

Two presets: Graphite when the operating system is dark, Paper when it is light. Dango follows the setting on the next paint, without a restart. There is no theme file and no theme picker. A new theme would be one more preset value, with no component change. The theme tweak here only previews the two.

States and keys

What root search shows, and what Escape does at each stage. One state machine in Rust decides all of it; the UI only renders.

Root stateWhat showsKeys
Bare pillEmpty query, nothing pinned. The pill alone: no card, no ActionBar, no selection.↓ opens the list at its first row. ↑, ↵ and {{ k.panel }} do nothing.
Needs attentionEmpty query with pinned items, such as an update or a missing permission. The card shows only them under “Needs attention”, first one selected.↓ opens the full list and selects the row after the pinned one.
Empty-query listOpened with ↓. “Needs attention”, then “Suggestions” (at most 6), then “Everything else”. Flat, without headings, when nothing is pinned or launched yet.↑ ↓ move. Typing replaces it with ranked results.
Typed queryOne ranked list, no headings. The card appears when the first result for the query lands. An answer card may sit on top.↑ ↓ move, ↵ runs the primary action, {{ k.panel }} opens the panel.
No resultsEmptyView “No results for” and the query, only once every provider has answered.Escape clears the query.

Escape, stage by stage

  1. 1ActionPanel open → closes only the panel.
  2. 2Pushed view → pops it, with or without text in the prompt.
  3. 3Root with text → clears the query; the closed root view shows.
  4. 4Empty query, list open → closes the list.
  5. 5Bare pill or Needs attention card → hides the launcher and resets it.

The global shortcut, a click on another app, and a click in the gap hide the launcher too, but hold its state for a grace period: shown again in time, it comes back as it was.

Screens

Each screen at its real size: 720 wide, pill 52, gap 8, card up to 480 in total. A list or grid card is as tall as its rows; a list with a detail pane, a detail, a form, a progress view and an empty view take the full 480. The pill holds a query for lists and grids and the command’s title otherwise. The ActionBar’s left slot is the extension of the selected result at root, and the command in a pushed view.

LauncherWindow

shell

Purpose: the root. One borderless, always-on-top window with a transparent background, built once at startup and only shown and hidden after. It holds the pill and the card as two rounded shapes; what is behind the launcher shows through the gap and around the corners. A click on a transparent part counts as a click outside and hides the launcher. Height: pill, gap and card together are at most 480. On Windows the window follows the visible launcher, and while results still arrive it grows but does not shrink. On macOS the window stays 480 tall and everything below the card is transparent.

shell

Purpose: the pill. It holds the query for a list or grid, with a placeholder the view sets (“Search apps and commands” at root, “Search” in pushed views). Filter dropdown

shell

Purpose: narrow a pushed list or grid. When the view declares a filter, the SearchBar shows the chosen option as a pill at its right end. A click opens a menu of the options below it. The same options also appear in the ActionPanel under “Filter”, with ✓ on the current one, so the keyboard reaches them through {{ k.panel }}. Choosing one keeps the query and hands the option to the view’s command. Root search has no filter.

Toast

shell

Purpose: status without taking focus. One component, two places: in the ActionBar’s left slot while the card shows, and at the pill’s right end (at most half its width) while only the pill shows. It never changes the launcher’s size; when the card opens, a toast still showing moves into the ActionBar. Long text is cut off with an ellipsis. It may carry one action, shown after the text as its label and chord; pressing the chord runs only that action. Typing is never interrupted. After the launcher hides, the HUD reports instead.

ActionPanel

overlay

Purpose: every action on the selection, opened with {{ k.panel }} or by clicking “Actions”. It floats above the ActionBar at the card’s right edge, with a shadow, and the card grows to make room. While open it owns navigation, typing and confirming: typing goes to its filter at the bottom, ↵ runs one action, Escape or a click outside closes only the panel and the selection below stays. Actions are grouped by section, the view’s filter options follow under “Filter”, and danger actions come last in the danger role. A long list scrolls and keeps the selected action in view.

View stack

state machine

Purpose: running a command can push its view on top of root search. Escape or the back control pops one view, whether or not the prompt holds text, and the previous view comes back with its query and selection intact. Pushed views always show both the pill and the card.

HUD

own window

Purpose: report an outcome the user cannot see because the launcher is hidden: a failure, a copy that closed the launcher, or a background task that finished or needs input. It looks like a toast on its own: a dot and one line in a pill that fits its text, up to 560 wide. It shows centred near the bottom of the work area of the display under the pointer, over every window, and never takes focus or clicks. Success leaves after about 2 s, failure after about 4 s; a newer HUD replaces the old one, and showing the launcher removes it.

Atoms

primitives

The smallest pieces, in ui/atoms/, reused by everything above. Named icons are Lucide, from one registry in src/slint_shell/icons.rs; nothing else draws an icon.

Icon
20 px, square or round. A named icon (icon:<name>) is drawn in a theme role, muted by default. An app icon or favicon keeps its colours and aspect ratio. A content preview, such as a copied image, fills a framed square. Unknown: a raised square.
{{ k.panel }}{{ k.cmdEnter }}{{ k.hotkey }}
Kbd
A whole chord in one chip: Geist Mono 12, muted, raised fill, border, radius 4. Platform names: on Windows keys separated by a space (“Ctrl K”), on macOS joined (“⌘K”). One format everywhere.

Accessory
Text (muted 12), icon, tag, Kbd, progress ring (16 px) or status dot. A row shows at most three, right-aligned.
Working Needs input Done Failed Queued
Dot
6 px, never moves. Five states, each in its own role. As an accessory it tells assistive technology its state.
Progress
Bar or ring, working fill on a raised track. Determinate only when the total is known; otherwise it moves without an amount. A spinner is a small indeterminate ring. The SearchBar’s loading bar is the one exception (accent on border).
Skeleton
Rows of row height with raised shapes where icon and title go, as many as fit the full launcher. Not selectable, not read out. Only for a loading list with no items.

Loading and progress

pattern

Purpose: show busy work by scope. Smaller scope, quieter mark. Typing, Escape and {{ k.panel }} keep working throughout.

What is busySurface
The shown view is loadingSearchBar loading bar.
A list with nothing yetSkeleton rows, replaced by the items in the same frame they arrive.
Root search still answeringThe window grows with results but does not shrink, and “No results” waits, until every provider has answered.
A command from root searchBusy toast “Running” and the command’s title, then “Loading…” if its view loads.
One itemProgress ring or status dot as the row’s accessory.
A background taskBusy toast with its own line, never the loading bar. It survives hiding the launcher; the HUD reports when it ends while hidden.
A multi-step jobProgressView, such as an update download.

Rules

  1. 1No blinking the busy marks appear only after about 150 ms of work and then stay at least about 400 ms. Replacing the view while still busy does not restart them.
  2. 2Honest numbers determinate only when the total is known; never a fake percentage.
  3. 3Never take focus a toast never interrupts typing.
  4. 4Leaving clears at once leaving the view or a reset removes the marks in the same frame.
  5. 5Tasks outlive views back, hide and the grace period do not cancel a background task.
